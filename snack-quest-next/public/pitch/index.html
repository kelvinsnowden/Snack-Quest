01The opportunity

Kenyans discover global snack culture online, and then can’t buy it.

TikTok, Instagram and YouTube put Japanese, Korean, American, British and European snacks in front of Kenyan consumers every day. The demand is created online. The supply has not caught up.

Why buying it today is hard

  • Fragmented. A handful of specialty sellers scattered across platforms.
  • Inconsistent. Stock appears and disappears without warning.
  • Hard to discover. No single place to browse what exists.
  • Badly suited to impulse. Social discovery is instant; buying is not.
  • Opaque. Prices vary widely between informal sellers.

What Snack Quest is

Not a snack-box company. A consumer commerce and retail brand where the product is the experience — discovery, novelty, culture, gifting, and something worth filming.

Physical retail E-commerce, live Curated boxes Creator-led discovery Gifting Brand distribution (future)

The loop the whole business runs on

Discovery online → trust in store → repeat through the box → replicated location by location. The store acquires customers and removes the payment objection. The box is why they come back and what raises order value. The format is designed so location two is a build, not an experiment.

Two numbers we are not leaving for you to find

Repeat purchase is zero. Fourteen orders, fourteen different customers, and the oldest of them is fifteen days old. The box is our retention thesis and it has not been tested yet.

Seven boxes went out free on 1 September — roughly KSh 30,500 of stock at list price, seeded to creators. They are excluded from every figure on this slide. They carry real stock against no sale, and counting them as orders would drag the average order value from KSh 4,254 down to KSh 2,836 while adding nothing to revenue.

And the margin, as recorded

25.9% gross across the four orders with itemised goods cost, against a 45% target. 21.3% contribution across all nine orders with any cost recorded — after delivery and packaging. Order-level contribution ranged from −21% to +39%, and one early order sold below cost. It moves as more shopping trips are costed: 19.7% over the first four orders, 19.0% over seven, 21.3% now.

Eleven orders is a small number and we are not going to dress it up. It is here because the shape matters more than the size: order value is ahead of plan, margin is behind it, and we can tell you which by exactly how much. 04The constraint

Three constraints. Not one of them is demand.

Two are solved by a location and one by having stock money on the table. None is a question of whether people want the product — which is exactly what makes them fixable with this raise rather than with a better idea.

One — trust and payment friction

We lose sales to customers who want to pay on delivery, or who simply will not pay online before they trust the brand. 177,800 watched in 28 days; fourteen have bought in total.

A physical location is a place to hand over cash and take the box away. It removes the objection entirely, and the collection split measures whether that is true.

Two — margin, and where it leaks

We record 25.9% gross against a 45% target. The gap is not pricing, and it is not delivery — delivery is a known formula under the Tushop terms.

It is landed cost. We buy locally at close to retail and resell. Buying direct from Korea is the single lever, and it only works at volume.

Three — an open door, and nothing to trade

Exotic Snack Guys run this format in the United States: 100+ locations, 49,600 followers, features on FOX, WGN, Yahoo and the Chicago Tribune, and a bio that reads DM for collabs. We approached them, they asked how they could help, and a proposal for a joint first opening is with them now.

Creators want product, not cash — and we have no space to host, shoot and broadcast at that volume.

The strategic point, stated plainly

Asking an established brand to put its name beside ours is asking them to take a risk. Asking them to supply us at volume, paid upfront, is asking them to do business. The second is the far smaller ask and the better first step — and stock money is what turns a favour into a transaction. That is true of Exotic Snack Guys and it is true of the Korean line. The opportunity already exists; what is missing is the infrastructure to serve it and the cash to be worth dealing with.

05Operating spine
Partners, not headcount

Product in, orders out — without building either.

The two things that usually sink a small retail operation are getting stock and getting it to the customer. Both are handled by named partners rather than by hiring into them. The company is two people.

Supply — direct Korean sourcing

A supplier relationship that will source any snack from Korea and ship it to Kenya on a two-to-three month lead time.

It changes what the catalogue can be: instead of selling whatever is already importable into Kenya, we can chase what customers are actually asking for on TikTok — including the products driving a trend before anyone else here stocks them. Stated as it stands: an established relationship, not an exclusive contract.

Delivery — Tushop, and what its terms do

Third-party logistics covering Nairobi and roughly 30km around it. Express in 90 minutes; same day if logged by 1pm; next day if logged by midnight.

Today the company is two people. Tushop is the delivery team we would otherwise have to raise money to hire — no riders, no fleet, no capex, monthly invoicing.

Delivery is a formula, not a guess KSh 35/km + 3% KSh 35 per kilometre of optimised route, plus 3% of declared order value for goods insurance. That goes straight into the model rather than being estimated — and it means the cost of serving a customer is known before we accept the order.
Batching rewards density 3 drops, one fee Three same-day orders sharing one optimised 20km route cost the same KSh 700 as a single drop on that route — not KSh 700 each. Delivery cost per order therefore falls as order density rises, so a store that concentrates demand in one part of Nairobi makes every delivery cheaper.
Insured in transit 100% of value The full declared order value is covered while goods are in Tushop’s custody.
Live customer tracking Built in The customer gets a tracking link and the rider’s details, then a delivery confirmation and a review prompt — the review engine on a business with no reviews yet.

Tushop’s terms and service levels are as set out above and the teams are aligning for kickoff. Both partner relationships are described exactly as they stand today, with no implied exclusivity.

Gifting is the engine

Somewhere in this city, today is somebody’s birthday — and tomorrow it is somebody else’s. Add Valentine’s, Christmas, anniversaries, apologies and the surprise sent for no reason. Gifting does not require the buyer to be a snack enthusiast, which makes it the widest door in. Our measured KSh 4,254 order is already gift-sized, not pocket money.

Campus, and the content with it

Students are the audience already watching us: dense, social, and the people who make the format spread. A series filmed across Nairobi’s universities — where the visit is the acquisition and the content at the same time, the same trick the TikTok account already pulls for free.

Children, and the parents who pay

Odd flavours from other countries are a child’s idea of an event. Children pull, parents pay, and the occasion repeats: birthdays, school holidays, rewards, treats. The least fashionable segment here and probably the most reliable.

What only a shop can sell

Today the smallest thing anybody can buy from us is a box. A walk-in buying one packet is a customer we currently turn away, because we have no product at that price and no way to hand it over. That is demand we cannot serve at all — at a frequency a box will never have.

The honest reading. Six occasions a year is an assumption, not a result — repeat purchase is currently zero, and proving it is the whole point of Store #1. How the second inventory cycle is funded — the question most decks skip

The arithmetic. KSh 2.5M of landed stock at the 45% target margin supports roughly KSh 4.55M of revenue — about 1.5 months of stock at the KSh 3.0M target. The Korean lead time is two to three months.

So order two has to be placed close to opening, before sell-through data exists, and paid for when cash is tightest. Left unaddressed the shelves run thin around the end of month three with the second container still at sea — and the faster the store ramps, the sooner that happens. We would rather show you this than have you find it.

Three mitigations, in order

  • Stagger the first order. Split the KSh 2.5M across two sequenced shipments. Same capital, same total stock, smoothed arrivals — and the second tranche is committed knowing what actually sells.
  • Seek terms on order two — 50% deposit, 50% on delivery — which halves the cash needed at the tightest moment. One clean prepaid first order is the track record that earns them.
  • Local bridge stock if a gap still opens. Explicitly the fallback, not the plan, because it reopens the margin problem.

The three inventory, build and working-capital lines are KSh 4.5M of the KSh 5M, and that shape is the argument: this is a shop being stocked, opened and given six months to trade — not a marketing budget with a shopfront attached. There is deliberately no contingency line: at this size a contingency is just a smaller stock order, and stock is the thing that fixes the margin.
